# Hospital Negligence Lawsuits in New York: A Step-by-Step Guide

Hospitals are institutions — and suing an institution is meaningfully different from suing an individual physician. When negligence occurs within a hospital setting, multiple legal theories, multiple potential defendants, and significant procedural complexity come into play. This guide walks through each step of a New York hospital negligence lawsuit.

## Step 1: Understand Who Can Be Sued

In hospital negligence cases, the identity of the defendant matters enormously — both for the legal theories available and for the practical outcome.

### The Hospital Itself
Hospitals can be held directly liable for their own negligence:

- **Negligent hiring:** Hiring or retaining a physician or staff member with a known history of incompetence
- **Negligent supervision:** Failing to supervise residents, nurses, or other staff properly
- **Negligent credentialing:** Granting privileges to a physician who was not adequately qualified
- **Institutional protocols:** Inadequate policies for infection control, medication management, or patient monitoring
- **Systemic understaffing:** Operating below minimum safe staffing ratios, leading to preventable harm

### Vicarious Liability for Employees
Hospitals are vicariously liable for the negligence of their **employees** acting within the scope of their employment. Nurses, resident physicians, and hospital-employed attending physicians typically create vicarious liability.

**Key distinction:** Hospitals are generally NOT vicariously liable for **independent contractors** — which includes many attending physicians who have staff privileges at the hospital but are not on its payroll. Your attorney must determine the employment relationship for each involved provider.

### The Individual Physicians and Providers
Physicians, surgeons, anesthesiologists, radiologists, and nurses can be named as defendants individually alongside the hospital.

### Hospital Affiliates and Related Entities
Large hospital systems often have complex corporate structures: affiliated physician groups, separate management entities, and multiple legal entities operating within one physical location. Identifying all potentially liable entities requires investigation.

## Step 2: Investigate the Claim

Before any lawsuit can be filed, your attorney must conduct a thorough pre-suit investigation:

### Obtaining Medical Records
Patients and their authorized representatives have the right to all medical records. Key records in a hospital negligence case include admission and discharge summaries, nursing notes, physician orders and progress notes, operative reports, anesthesia records, medication administration records, and laboratory and imaging reports.

### Medical Expert Review
New York's **Certificate of Merit** requirement mandates that the plaintiff's attorney certify that a licensed medical professional has reviewed the case and found a reasonable basis for the claim.

### Identifying the Standard of Care Breach
The investigation must identify specifically what the hospital or provider did or failed to do that a reasonably competent provider would not have done.

## Step 3: File the Lawsuit

New York hospital negligence cases are filed in **Supreme Court** in the county where the malpractice occurred.

### Statute of Limitations
The general New York medical malpractice statute of limitations is **2.5 years (30 months)** from the date of the malpractice or from the last date of continuous treatment for the condition at issue.

### Notice of Claim for Public Hospitals
If the hospital is operated by a government entity (e.g., NYC Health + Hospitals), **a Notice of Claim must be filed within 90 days** of the malpractice. Failure to file a timely Notice of Claim bars the lawsuit entirely.

## Step 4: Discovery

Discovery in hospital negligence cases is extensive. Both sides exchange medical records, credentialing files, staffing records, hospital policies, and conduct depositions of all key witnesses including physicians, nurses, and hospital administrators.

## Step 5: Motion Practice

After discovery closes, defendants typically move for **summary judgment**. Successfully defeating summary judgment — particularly on the causation element — is often the decisive moment in hospital negligence litigation.

## Step 6: Trial

If the case does not settle, it proceeds to a jury trial in New York Supreme Court. Hospital negligence trials typically last 2–4 weeks. Damages may include past and future medical expenses, past and future lost earnings, pain and suffering (no statutory cap in New York), and wrongful death damages.
