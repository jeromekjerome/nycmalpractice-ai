# New Orleans Birth Injury Lawsuits: Louisiana Malpractice Law Explained

A birth injury can change a family's life in an instant. Whether caused by oxygen deprivation during delivery, improper use of forceps or vacuum extraction, delayed emergency intervention, or failure to respond to fetal distress signs, birth injuries often result in permanent conditions — cerebral palsy, Erb's palsy, brain damage, or developmental delays — that require a lifetime of care.

If your child suffered a birth injury at a New Orleans hospital or birthing center, Louisiana law may entitle you to substantial compensation. But Louisiana's medical malpractice statutes are among the most complex in the country, with specific procedural requirements, damage caps, and deadlines that differ significantly from other states.

This guide explains how **birth injury lawsuits work in Louisiana**, what you need to prove, and what steps to take first.

---

## Common Birth Injuries That May Give Rise to a Malpractice Claim

Not every birth complication is malpractice. A birth injury becomes actionable when a healthcare provider's conduct fell below the standard of care and that failure caused the injury.

Common birth injuries associated with malpractice claims in Louisiana include:

- **Cerebral palsy (CP)** — often caused by oxygen deprivation (hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy) during or immediately after delivery, which may result from delayed C-section, failure to monitor fetal heart rate, or mismanagement of umbilical cord complications
- **Erb's palsy / brachial plexus injuries** — damage to the nerve network controlling shoulder, arm, and hand function, often associated with shoulder dystocia mismanagement
- **Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE)** — brain injury from oxygen deprivation; may result from delayed delivery, mismanaged uterine rupture, or placental abruption not acted upon in time
- **Facial nerve damage** — caused by improper use of forceps
- **Neonatal infections** — caused by failure to identify or treat maternal infection (e.g., Group B strep) prior to delivery
- **Wrongful death** — in cases where the infant does not survive a preventable birth complication

## Louisiana's Medical Malpractice Framework

Louisiana has a unique dual-track system under the **Louisiana Medical Malpractice Act (LMMA)**.

### The Medical Review Panel

Before you can file suit in court, Louisiana law requires most malpractice claims be submitted to a **Medical Review Panel (MRP)** consisting of three physicians in the relevant specialty plus an attorney chairperson. The panel reviews evidence and issues an opinion on whether the defendant's conduct breached the standard of care.

The panel's opinion is admissible in subsequent litigation but is not binding. Even a panel opinion in the defendant's favor does not prevent you from filing suit.

The MRP process typically takes **12–18 months**. During this time, the lawsuit statute of limitations is suspended.

### Damages Cap in Louisiana Birth Injury Cases

Louisiana law caps total malpractice damages against a qualified healthcare provider at **$500,000** (exclusive of future medical care costs). However, **future medical care and related benefits** are paid by the Patient's Compensation Fund directly and are *not* subject to the $500,000 cap.

For severe birth injuries like cerebral palsy or HIE requiring lifetime care, the uncapped medical expenses portion can far exceed the cap — often reaching several million dollars in present value. Total recovery in a serious Louisiana birth injury case can be well above $500,000.

## Statute of Limitations

Louisiana's medical malpractice statute of limitations is **one year** from the date the claimant knew or should have known of the malpractice — with an absolute maximum of **three years** from the act of malpractice.

For birth injuries to a minor, the one-year clock does not begin running until the child reaches majority (18) if the injury was not discoverable earlier. However, the three-year repose period applies to the underlying act of malpractice, which can complicate late-discovered claims.

Given this complexity, if you believe your child's birth injury may have been caused by medical negligence, consult an attorney immediately.

## What You Need to Prove

To prevail in a Louisiana birth injury malpractice claim, you must establish:

1. **Duty** — the healthcare provider owed a duty of care
2. **Breach** — the provider's conduct fell below the standard of care
3. **Causation** — the breach caused the birth injury (frequently the most contested element)
4. **Damages** — the child and family suffered measurable harm

Expert testimony from obstetricians, neonatologists, and pediatric neurologists is typically required.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a deadline even if my child's diagnosis came years after birth?**
Yes. Louisiana's discovery rule starts the one-year clock when you knew or should have known about the malpractice.

**Does the $500,000 cap apply to all Louisiana birth injury cases?**
Only to qualified healthcare providers enrolled in the PCF. The cap does not apply to providers who are not PCF participants.

**Can I still file suit if the Medical Review Panel rules against me?**
Yes. The panel opinion is not binding. Many successful malpractice cases proceed to verdict despite adverse panel opinions.

**What if the baby died?**
A wrongful death claim may be available to the parents and siblings under Louisiana law. The same MRP process and damages cap structure applies.
