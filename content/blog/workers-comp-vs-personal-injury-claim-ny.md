# Workers Compensation vs. Personal Injury in New York: Which Claim to File

If you were injured at work in New York, you are likely entitled to workers' compensation benefits. But depending on how your injury occurred, you may also have the right to file a personal injury lawsuit against a third party — and the difference in potential recovery is substantial.

Understanding the distinction between **workers' compensation and personal injury claims in New York** is critical. Filing only a workers' comp claim when you have grounds for a lawsuit could cost you tens of thousands of dollars in damages you are legally owed.

---

## Workers' Compensation: The Baseline Protection

New York's workers' compensation system is a no-fault insurance program that provides benefits to employees injured on the job, regardless of who caused the accident. In exchange for these guaranteed benefits, employees generally give up their right to sue their employer for negligence.

### What Workers' Comp Covers

- **Medical expenses**: All reasonable and necessary treatment related to the work injury
- **Lost wages**: Two-thirds of your average weekly wage, subject to a state maximum, for the duration of your disability
- **Permanent disability benefits**: Lump sum or ongoing payments for permanent impairment
- **Death benefits**: Payments to surviving dependents

### What Workers' Comp Does NOT Cover

- Pain and suffering
- Full lost wages (capped at two-thirds, with a state maximum)
- Emotional distress
- Loss of consortium

This is the critical limitation. For serious injuries — spinal damage, traumatic brain injury, permanent disability — the gap between workers' comp benefits and full compensation can be enormous.

## Personal Injury Claims: When Can You Sue?

New York law bars employees from suing their own employer for a workplace injury (with limited exceptions for intentional acts). However, you can file a personal injury lawsuit against **third parties** — anyone other than your employer whose negligence contributed to your injury.

### Common Third-Party Claims After Workplace Injuries

**General contractor and property owner liability:** Under New York Labor Law Sections 240 and 241, property owners and general contractors have non-delegable duties to provide safe working conditions for construction workers. Violations of these "scaffold law" provisions can result in absolute liability — meaning a single statutory violation establishes fault without requiring proof of negligence.

**Equipment manufacturer liability:** If defective machinery, tools, or safety equipment caused your injury, the manufacturer may be liable under product liability law. This is separate from and in addition to any workers' comp claim.

**Negligent driver liability:** Delivery drivers, construction workers, and others injured in vehicle accidents while on the job may have both a workers' comp claim and a personal injury claim against the at-fault driver.

**Property owner negligence:** Workers injured on a third party's property — not their employer's — due to unsafe conditions may have premises liability claims.

## Filing Both Claims Simultaneously

New York law allows — and in many cases, requires — injured workers to file both a workers' compensation claim and a personal injury lawsuit when both apply. There is no prohibition on pursuing both remedies simultaneously.

However, there is a coordination mechanism: if you recover in a personal injury lawsuit, your employer's workers' compensation insurer typically has a **lien** on that recovery for the benefits already paid. This lien is negotiable and does not eliminate the financial benefit of the personal injury claim — a significant third-party recovery almost always exceeds workers' comp benefits for serious injuries.

## Why the Choice Matters: A Real-World Comparison

Consider a construction worker who suffers a severe back injury due to a scaffold collapse:

**Workers' comp only:** Two-thirds of weekly wages during recovery, medical bills covered, potential permanent disability award. Total recovery: $80,000-$150,000.

**Workers' comp plus Labor Law 240 lawsuit:** Workers' comp benefits plus pain and suffering, full lost earning capacity, and future care costs. Total recovery: $500,000-$2,000,000+.

The difference is not marginal. Failing to identify and pursue the third-party claim is one of the most costly mistakes injured New York workers make.

## Time Limits: Do Not Miss Your Window

- **Workers' comp claim**: Report your injury to your employer within **30 days** and file a claim with the Workers' Compensation Board within **2 years** of the accident or last payment of benefits.
- **Personal injury lawsuit**: New York's statute of limitations is **3 years** from the date of injury for most third-party claims.
- **Labor Law claims against municipalities**: A Notice of Claim must be filed within **90 days** if a city or municipal entity is involved.
