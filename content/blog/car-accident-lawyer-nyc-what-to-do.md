# Car Accident Lawyer NYC: What to Do in the First 24 Hours

A car accident in New York City can happen in seconds — a rear-end collision on the FDR Drive, a rideshare crash in Midtown, a pedestrian struck at a crosswalk in Brooklyn. In the chaos that follows, most people do not know that the decisions they make in the first 24 hours can dramatically affect whether they recover full compensation for their injuries.

This guide walks you through exactly what to do — and what not to do — immediately after a car accident in NYC, and explains how an experienced **car accident lawyer in NYC** can protect your rights from the start.

---

## Step 1: Prioritize Safety and Call 911

Your first obligation is safety. If anyone is injured, call 911 immediately. New York law requires drivers to report accidents involving injury, death, or property damage above $1,000. Do not move your vehicle unless it poses an active traffic hazard — the scene itself is evidence.

While waiting for police:
- Turn on your hazard lights
- Check yourself and passengers for injuries — adrenaline can mask pain
- Do not admit fault or apologize — anything you say can be used against you

## Step 2: Document Everything at the Scene

Before vehicles are moved or towed, collect as much documentation as possible:

- **Photos and video**: Capture damage to all vehicles, skid marks, road conditions, traffic signals, and any visible injuries
- **Driver information**: Name, license number, insurance carrier, policy number, and plate number for every driver involved
- **Witness information**: Names and phone numbers of anyone who saw the accident
- **Police report number**: Ask the responding officer for the report number so you can retrieve it later

If you were struck by an Uber, Lyft, taxi, or commercial vehicle, note the company name and vehicle identification number. These cases involve multiple insurance policies and require specific legal strategies.

## Step 3: Seek Medical Attention Immediately

Do not wait to see a doctor — even if you feel fine. Whiplash, traumatic brain injuries, and soft-tissue damage often do not produce immediate symptoms. Delaying treatment gives insurance companies ammunition to argue your injuries were pre-existing or unrelated to the accident.

In New York, your own auto insurance covers your initial medical bills under **No-Fault (PIP) coverage**, regardless of who caused the accident. You must file a No-Fault claim within **30 days** of the accident or risk losing this benefit.

For injuries that exceed No-Fault thresholds — what New York calls a serious injury under Insurance Law Section 5102(d) — you may be entitled to sue the at-fault driver for additional compensation including pain and suffering.

## Step 4: Report the Accident to Your Insurance Company

Notify your own insurer promptly, but be careful about what you say. Stick to the facts: date, time, location, and the other vehicle's information. Do not give a recorded statement or sign any releases without speaking to a lawyer first.

New York is a **no-fault state**, which means your own insurance pays your medical bills and lost wages up to policy limits regardless of fault. But no-fault does not cover pain and suffering — for that, you must pursue a liability claim against the at-fault driver.

## Step 5: Consult a NYC Car Accident Lawyer Before Accepting Any Settlement

Insurance adjusters are trained to minimize payouts. They may call you within days of the accident offering a quick settlement — often far less than your case is worth. Once you accept, you typically sign away your right to pursue further compensation, even if your injuries worsen.

A **car accident lawyer in NYC** can:
- Investigate the accident and preserve critical evidence
- Identify all liable parties, including municipalities for road defects
- Calculate your full damages — medical bills, lost wages, future care, and pain and suffering
- Negotiate aggressively with insurance companies
- File a lawsuit if a fair settlement cannot be reached

New York's statute of limitations for personal injury is **three years** from the date of the accident. For municipal defendants, you may have as little as 90 days to file a Notice of Claim. Do not wait.

## Common NYC Car Accident Scenarios

**Rideshare accidents:** Coverage depends on whether the Uber or Lyft driver was logged in, waiting for a ride, or actively transporting a passenger at the time of the crash. Multiple policies may apply, and navigating them requires experience.

**Pedestrian and cyclist accidents:** NYC's density makes pedestrian knockdown cases common. If struck in a crosswalk, fault analysis typically favors the pedestrian — but comparative negligence can still reduce your award.

**Commercial vehicle accidents:** Truck, delivery van, and bus accidents often involve corporate defendants with large insurance policies and aggressive defense teams. Thorough scene documentation is essential.

**Hit and run accidents:** New York's Motor Vehicle Accident Indemnification Corporation may cover injuries from uninsured or hit-and-run drivers. Strict reporting deadlines apply.

## What NOT to Do After a NYC Car Accident

- **Do not post about the accident on social media.** Defense attorneys routinely use social posts to undermine injury claims.
- **Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer** without your lawyer present.
- **Do not accept the first settlement offer** — initial offers rarely reflect full compensation.
- **Do not skip follow-up medical appointments.** Gaps in treatment are used to argue your injuries resolved.
