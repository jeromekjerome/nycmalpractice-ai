# Construction Accident Lawsuits in New York: What Injured Workers Need to Know

Construction work is one of the most dangerous occupations in the United States, and New York City's constant building activity means injuries are a daily reality. If you were hurt on a construction site in New York, understanding your legal rights is critical.

## Why New York Construction Accident Law Is Different

New York has some of the most worker-protective construction accident laws in the country. Three statutes govern most claims:

- **Labor Law §240 (The Scaffold Law):** Holds owners and general contractors strictly liable for gravity-related injuries — falls from scaffolding, ladders, elevated surfaces, or objects falling onto workers.
- **Labor Law §241(6):** Requires owners and contractors to provide reasonable and adequate protection for construction workers.
- **Labor Law §200:** The general duty statute requiring safe working conditions.

The scaffold law imposes absolute (strict) liability on property owners and general contractors — a worker's comparative fault is generally not a defense.

## Common Construction Accident Injuries in NYC

- Falls from scaffolding, ladders, or elevated platforms
- Falling objects: tools, materials, and debris striking workers below
- Electrical accidents
- Crane and heavy equipment accidents
- Trench and excavation collapses

## Who Is Liable in a New York Construction Accident?

1. **Property owners** — often liable under Labor Law §240 and §241 regardless of direct involvement
2. **General contractors** — have non-delegable duties to maintain safe conditions
3. **Subcontractors** — may carry liability for specific trade-related hazards they created
4. **Equipment manufacturers** — product liability if defective machinery contributed

Critical nuance: workers' compensation bars you from suing your direct employer, but does not bar claims against property owners, general contractors, or other third parties.

## What Compensation Can You Recover?

- Medical expenses (past and future)
- Lost wages and loss of earning capacity
- Pain and suffering
- Disfigurement

Scaffolding and fall cases under Labor Law §240 frequently result in seven-figure verdicts and settlements.

## The Statute of Limitations

- General personal injury: **3 years** from the accident date
- City of New York property: **Notice of Claim within 90 days**
- Wrongful death: 2-year statute of limitations
