# Louisiana Malpractice Statute of Limitations: The 1-Year Prescriptive Period

Louisiana has one of the strictest medical malpractice statutes of limitations in the United States. Understanding the **1-year prescriptive period** is critical—missing this deadline can permanently bar your claim.

## The Basic Rule: One Year from Discovery

In Louisiana, you have **one year from the date you discover (or should have discovered) the malpractice** to file a lawsuit. This is called a "prescriptive period," not a statute of limitations.

### The 1-Year Clock Starts When:
- You discover the malpractice injury, OR
- You reasonably should have discovered it through reasonable diligence

### Example
If a surgical tool is left inside your body but discovered 18 months after surgery, your one-year clock begins when discovered—not when surgery occurred.

## The Outer Time Limit: Three Years

There is an **absolute 3-year limit** from the date of the alleged negligent act, regardless of discovery. After 3 years, your claim is barred with rare exceptions.

### Timeline Summary
- **Standard deadline**: 1 year from discovery of injury
- **Absolute cutoff**: 3 years from date of malpractice act
- **Whichever comes first** = your deadline

## Exceptions & Limited Extensions

Louisiana recognizes limited exceptions:

**Continuous Treatment Doctrine**: If your healthcare provider continues treating the condition created by the negligence, the clock may not start until treatment ends.

**Fraudulent Concealment**: If the provider actively concealed the malpractice, additional time may be granted.

**Minority/Incapacity**: If you're a minor or legally incapacitated, the clock may be extended.

## Why This Deadline Is Critical

Louisiana's strict 1-year prescriptive period is much shorter than most states (typically 2-3 years). Delays in seeking medical opinion or legal advice can result in loss of your right to recover.
