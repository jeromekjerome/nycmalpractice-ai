Statute of Limitations — Critical Warning

The medical malpractice statute of limitations in New York (CPLR §214-a) is generally 2 years and 6 months from the act or omission. Special rules apply:

  • Continuous treatment doctrine: SOL is tolled while the same provider treats you for the same condition
  • Foreign object: If an object was left in the body, the claim may be brought within 1 year of its discovery (or of when it reasonably should have been discovered), even after the 2 years and 6 months have run. Implants, fixation devices and chemical compounds do not count as foreign objects.
  • Wrongful death: 2 years from date of death (EPTL §5-4.1) — shorter than personal injury SOL
  • Infancy toll: For patients who were minors, the deadline is extended, but in malpractice cases never beyond 10 years after the malpractice (CPLR §208)
  • Discovery rule: Applies in only two situations: a foreign object (above), and a failure to diagnose cancer or a malignant tumor (Lavern’s Law, 2018), where the 2 years and 6 months run from when the patient knew or reasonably should have known of the error and the harm, but no later than 7 years after it
  • Public hospitals: Claims against NYC Health + Hospitals (such as Bellevue, Kings County or Elmhurst) or another city- or county-run hospital need a notice of claim within 90 days, and the lawsuit must be filed within 1 year and 90 days

This platform does not provide a definitive SOL analysis. The AI may flag SOL concerns based on what you tell it, but it cannot definitively determine whether your claim is timely. Only a licensed attorney can make that determination.
