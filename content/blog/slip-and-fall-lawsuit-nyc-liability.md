# Slip and Fall Accidents in NYC: Building Owner Liability Explained

Every year, thousands of New Yorkers are injured in slip and fall accidents — on icy sidewalks, wet supermarket floors, broken building staircases, and defective sidewalk grates. Many assume they have no recourse. In reality, New York's premises liability laws impose clear duties on property owners, and violations of those duties can be grounds for a **slip and fall lawsuit in NYC**.

This guide explains how liability works, what you need to prove, and what steps to take to protect your claim.

---

## What Is Premises Liability in New York?

New York law requires property owners, managers, and occupiers to maintain their premises in a reasonably safe condition. When a dangerous condition exists and the responsible party knew — or should have known — about it and failed to correct it, they can be held liable for resulting injuries.

This duty applies broadly:
- **Residential building landlords** must maintain common areas, hallways, stairs, and entryways
- **Commercial property owners** — stores, restaurants, offices — must keep customer-accessible areas free of hazards
- **Municipal property** — sidewalks adjacent to commercial buildings are generally the property owner's responsibility under NYC Administrative Code Section 7-210
- **Construction sites** must comply with New York Labor Law protections for workers and passersby

## The Three Elements You Must Prove

To win a **slip and fall lawsuit in NYC**, you generally need to establish three things:

### 1. A Dangerous Condition Existed
You must show that a hazardous condition — a wet floor, broken stair, ice patch, torn carpet, uneven sidewalk — was present at the time of your fall. Photos taken at the scene are critical evidence.

### 2. The Owner Knew or Should Have Known About It
This is often the most contested element. Courts look at:
- **Actual notice**: Did the owner know about the condition? Was there a prior complaint, maintenance request, or incident report?
- **Constructive notice**: Had the condition existed long enough that a reasonable owner should have discovered and fixed it? A puddle present for two hours carries more weight than one that appeared minutes before your fall.

### 3. The Condition Caused Your Injuries
You must connect the fall to your injuries with medical documentation. Seeking prompt medical attention — even if injuries seem minor — is essential both for your health and your legal case.

## NYC-Specific Liability Rules

**Sidewalk liability:** Under NYC Administrative Code 7-210, property owners — not the City — are generally responsible for maintaining the sidewalk abutting their property. If you trip on a broken sidewalk outside a commercial building, the building owner may be liable. Residential one-, two-, and three-family homes occupied by the owner are typically exempt.

**Snow and ice:** New York City gives property owners **four hours after snowfall stops** (or until 11 AM if snowfall ends between 9 PM and 7 AM) to clear sidewalks. After that window, the owner may be liable for ice and snow injuries.

**"Open and obvious" defense:** Defendants often argue the hazard was so visible that any reasonable person would have noticed and avoided it. However, New York courts recognize that visible hazards can still be actionable if the owner failed to remedy them and the plaintiff had no reasonable alternative path.

**Comparative negligence:** New York follows pure comparative fault rules. Even if you were partially at fault — say, you were looking at your phone when you fell — you can still recover damages, reduced by your percentage of fault.

## What to Do After a Slip and Fall in NYC

**At the scene:**
- Photograph the hazard, surrounding area, and your injuries before anything is cleaned up or repaired
- Report the incident to the property manager, store manager, or building superintendent
- Get the names and contact information of any witnesses
- Seek medical attention the same day

**In the following days:**
- Preserve your clothing and footwear — defendants sometimes argue the victim's footwear contributed to the fall
- Request copies of incident reports filed by the property
- Avoid giving recorded statements to the property owner's insurer without legal advice

## Time Limits for NYC Slip and Fall Claims

New York's statute of limitations for personal injury is **three years** from the date of the accident. However, if you were injured on **New York City or municipal property**, you must file a Notice of Claim within **90 days** of the accident — a strict prerequisite to any lawsuit. Missing this deadline typically bars your claim entirely.

Acting quickly is critical. Surveillance footage, witness memories, and physical conditions change rapidly. An early investigation by your legal team can preserve evidence that would otherwise disappear.
