# Construction Accident Lawsuits in New York: What Injured Workers Need to Know

Construction work is one of the most dangerous occupations in the United States, and New York City's constant building activity means injuries are a daily reality. If you were hurt on a construction site in New York, understanding your legal rights is critical — and acting quickly can make the difference between a strong case and a missed opportunity.

## Why New York Construction Accident Law Is Different

New York has some of the most worker-protective construction accident laws in the country. Three statutes govern most claims:

- **Labor Law §240 (The Scaffold Law):** Holds owners and general contractors strictly liable for gravity-related injuries — falls from scaffolding, ladders, elevated surfaces, or objects falling onto workers.
- **Labor Law §241(6):** Requires owners and contractors to provide reasonable and adequate protection for construction workers, incorporating OSHA standards into the legal framework.
- **Labor Law §200:** The general duty statute requiring safe working conditions — parallels common law negligence.

The scaffold law is unique in the U.S. — it imposes absolute (strict) liability on property owners and general contractors, meaning a worker's comparative fault is generally not a defense. This makes New York one of the most favorable jurisdictions in the country for injured construction workers.

## Common Construction Accident Injuries in NYC

- **Falls from scaffolding, ladders, or elevated platforms** — often the most serious
- **Falling objects:** Tools, materials, and debris striking workers below
- **Electrical accidents:** Contact with unshielded wiring or faulty equipment
- **Crane and heavy equipment accidents**
- **Trench and excavation collapses**

High-injury worksites in NYC are concentrated in Manhattan, Brooklyn, and Queens — particularly around Hudson Yards, the South Bronx, and Long Island City.

## Who Is Liable in a New York Construction Accident?

Liability can extend beyond your direct employer:

1. **Property owners** — often liable under Labor Law §240 and §241 regardless of direct involvement
2. **General contractors** — have non-delegable duties to maintain safe conditions
3. **Subcontractors** — may carry liability for specific trade-related hazards they created
4. **Equipment manufacturers** — product liability if defective machinery contributed to the injury
5. **Third-party contractors** — workers on multi-contractor sites can sue other contractors whose negligence caused the harm

Critical nuance: workers' compensation bars you from suing your direct employer, but does not bar claims against property owners, general contractors, or other third parties. This is the pathway most injured workers use to pursue full compensation.

## What Compensation Can You Recover?

A successful construction accident lawsuit in New York can recover:

- **Medical expenses:** Past and future treatment, surgeries, rehabilitation, assistive devices
- **Lost wages:** Income you were unable to earn during recovery
- **Loss of earning capacity:** If the injury permanently affects your ability to work
- **Pain and suffering:** Non-economic damages for physical pain and emotional distress
- **Disfigurement:** Compensation for permanent scarring or physical changes

Scaffolding and fall cases under Labor Law §240 frequently result in seven-figure verdicts and settlements.

## The Statute of Limitations: Do Not Wait

- General personal injury: **3 years** from the date of the accident
- City of New York property: **Notice of Claim within 90 days** — missing this permanently bars the claim
- Wrongful death: 2-year statute of limitations

## Steps to Take After a Construction Accident

1. Seek medical attention immediately
2. Report the accident to your supervisor and ensure an incident report is filed
3. Document everything: photographs of the scene, your injuries, equipment involved
4. Collect witness information
5. Do not give statements to insurance adjusters without consulting an attorney
6. Preserve physical evidence
